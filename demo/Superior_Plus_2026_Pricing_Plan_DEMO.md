# Superior Plus Propane — 2026 Pricing Plan

> **SYNTHETIC DEMO DOCUMENT — NOT AN APPROVED SUPERIOR PLUS POLICY**  
> Created solely to demonstrate Google Drive retrieval and cross-domain analysis in Genie One. All objectives and guardrails below are illustrative.

**Document owner:** Commercial Strategy — Demo  
**Planning period:** 2026  
**Demo scope:** New York and New Jersey residential propane accounts  
**Units:** USD and gallons  
**Status:** Illustrative working draft

## 1. Pricing objective

Improve contribution margin through targeted 2026 price actions while limiting avoidable customer attrition. Pricing decisions should reflect both market position and the customer's recent experience with Superior Plus.

A blanket increase across the entire book is not the intended strategy. The commercial goal is to distinguish accounts that can reasonably absorb an increase from accounts that require review or protection because pricing pressure is compounded by service friction.

## 2. Customer-protection guardrails

Before recommending a price increase, review the strongest available evidence across sales, customer experience, delivery reliability, and pricing.

- Treat a recent **verified runout or late delivery** as material service-recovery evidence. A transcript mention alone does not verify a delivery failure.
- Treat a customer **rate objection** as stronger evidence when it affected progression or disposition, rather than when price was merely mentioned.
- Consider repeated contacts, high hold time, abandonment, or poor CSAT as customer-experience friction; do not assume any one signal proves churn risk.
- Review accounts quoted above a known competitor rate or carrying a material fee load, especially when a rate objection or service failure is also present.
- Accounts with stable service, limited customer friction, and a competitive pricing position may be candidates for a price action, subject to commercial review.
- Do not represent a rule-based risk tier as a predicted probability of churn.
- Require human approval before applying a price change or initiating customer outreach.

These guardrails intentionally do not prescribe one permanent “Protect” formula. The decision owner should state how the term was operationalized for the analysis and expose the evidence behind each recommendation.

## 3. Evidence standard

Every account-level recommendation should distinguish:

1. **Verified facts** supported by governed source records.
2. **Inferred recommendations** derived from those facts.
3. **Missing evidence** that prevents a stronger conclusion.
4. **Synthetic or illustrative inputs** used in the demo.

Lost opportunity does not necessarily mean churn. A customer mentioning an issue does not necessarily mean that issue caused the loss. Where sales-call evidence is used, preserve the blocker disposition and supporting customer quote.

## 4. Recommended decision output

For each pricing review cycle, produce:

- A concise **Can Raise versus Protect** summary.
- A ranked Protect cohort using contribution at risk as a prioritization aid.
- An evidence chain covering sales, CX, delivery, and pricing for high-priority accounts.
- Explicit assumptions and missing-source warnings.
- Recommended next actions for human review.

## 5. Review cadence

Refresh the pricing-protection brief weekly using current governed data and the current version of this document. Do not reuse prior account lists or metrics without refreshing them. The brief is decision support; Sales/Ops and Finance retain approval authority.
